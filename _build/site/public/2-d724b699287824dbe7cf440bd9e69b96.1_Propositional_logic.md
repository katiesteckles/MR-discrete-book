# Propositional Logic

Logic deals with the truth, falsehood and consistency of formalised statements. Propositional logic is based around the idea of propositions.

```{admonition} Definition 2.1 - Proposition
:label: proposition-definition

A **proposition** is any sentence or statement that can be assigned the value $\texttt{true}$ (T) or the value \texttt{false} (F), but **not both**.

```

For example, the following are all propositions:
- $2 > 1$
- $\{1,2,3\} \cap \{3,4,5\} = \{3\}$
- The sum of any two even numbers is divisible by $3$.

Propositions are commonly denoted by the letters $p$, $q$ and $r$, which are referred to as **propositional variables**. Propositional variables can only assume (or be assigned) one of two values: \texttt{true} or \texttt{false}. 

```{admonition} Definition 2.2 - Atomic proposition
:label: atomic-proposition-definition

An **atomic proposition** or **simple proposition** is one which cannot be expressed in terms of simpler propositions. The propositional constants **T** and **F** are atomic propositions, as are propositional variables such as $p$, $q$ and $r$.

```

We refer to the **truth value** of a proposition, if known, to describe whether it is true or false. The **propositional constants** **T** and **F** are the atomic propositions assigned to the values \texttt{true} and \texttt{false} (respectively).


````{admonition} Example 2.1
:label: propositions-example
:class: hint

Which of the following are propositions?

&emsp; $p$: The sun is shining

&emsp; $q$: What time is it?

&emsp; $r$: Manchester is in the Northwest of England.

&emsp; $s$: Read this carefully.

&emsp; $t$: $x+2=4$

```{dropdown} Solution

$p$ and $r$ are propositions.

$q$ and $s$ are not propositions as they are not declarative statements.

$t$ is not a proposition because it cannot be assigned a value of true or false until we know the value of $x$.

```
````
