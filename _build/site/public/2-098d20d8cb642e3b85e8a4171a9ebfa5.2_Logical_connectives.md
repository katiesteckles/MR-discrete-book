We can combine existing propositions to produce new ones. These methods were introduced by the English mathematician George
Boole in 1854 in his book The Laws of Thought. Many mathematical statements are constructed by combining one or more propositions.


```{admonition} Definition 2.3 - Compound Proposition
:label: proposition-definition

A **compound proposition** is one which has been formed from two or more atomic propositions using logical operators.

The logical value of a compound proposition depends upon the truth values of its component atomic propositions.

```

All non-atomic propositions are compound propositions.

For example: ‘I am a student at university, and I am studying for a maths degree’ is a compound proposition, . It is constructed from two atomic propositions: ‘I am a student at university’ and ‘I am studying for a maths degree’.

The word 'and' which, connects the two propositions, is called a **logical connective**. All compound propositions contain
at least one logical connective.
